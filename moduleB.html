What is Simple Harmonic Motion?

Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) is the most fundamental type of periodic motion, where a restoring force always acts toward the equilibrium position and is directly proportional to the displacement. It appears everywhere — from the atoms in a crystal lattice to the pendulum of a clock, from guitar strings to the electrons in a radio antenna.
F = −kx ⟹ mẍ + kx = 0
k = spring constant (N/m) · x = displacement · m = mass Solution: x(t) = A·cos(ωt + φ) where ω = √(k/m)
The negative sign is the key: the force always opposes the displacement. If the block is pulled right (+x), the spring pulls it left (−F). This creates oscillation.

Core Equations

📍

Displacement

Position as a function of time — a cosine wave.
x(t) = A·cos(ωt + φ)
⚡

Velocity

Velocity is max at equilibrium, zero at extremes.
v(t) = −Aω·sin(ωt + φ)
🔄

Acceleration

Always opposite to displacement; max at extremes.
a(t) = −Aω²·cos(ωt)
⏱️

Period

Depends only on mass and spring constant, NOT amplitude!
T = 2π√(m/k)
💡

Angular Frequency

How fast the block oscillates in radians per second.
ω = √(k/m) = 2π/T
⚖️

Energy

Total mechanical energy is conserved; converts KE ↔ PE.
E = ½kA² = ½mv² + ½kx²

Scenario Zoo 🦁

SHM appears in many different physical configurations. Here are the key cases:
🧱

Free Oscillation

Ideal mass-spring with no friction. Energy is perfectly conserved; oscillation never stops.
ω = √(k/m)
🌊

Damped Oscillation

Viscous friction or air resistance removes energy. Amplitude decays exponentially.
x = Ae^(−bt/2m)cos(ω_d·t)
📦📦

Stacked Blocks

A second block sits on top. If inter-block friction is insufficient, the top block slides — changing the effective mass in the equation.
f_max = μ·m₂·ω²·A
⬇️

Vertical Spring

Gravity just shifts the equilibrium position downward by mg/k. The SHM around new equilibrium is identical to horizontal case!
x_eq = mg/k (shift)
🔗

Coupled Springs

Two masses connected by springs exhibit normal modes — symmetric and antisymmetric — with different frequencies.
ω₁=√(k/m), ω₂=√(3k/m)
🏔️

Dry Friction

Coulomb friction (floor friction) creates a deadband near equilibrium — the block stops before returning to x = 0.
x_stop = ±μmg/k

The Damping Spectrum

✨

Underdamped

b² < 4mk — oscillates with decreasing amplitude. Seen in car suspensions (just barely), guitar strings.
ω_d = √(ω₀²−(b/2m)²)
⚖️

Critically Damped

b² = 4mk — returns to equilibrium fastest without oscillating. Ideal for doors, shock absorbers.
b_c = 2√(mk)
🐌

Overdamped

b² > 4mk — returns slowly without oscillating. Like a piston in thick oil.
x(t)=e^(−bt/2m)(C₁e^γt+C₂e^−γt)
